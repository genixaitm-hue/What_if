# Fact-Check Notes — "Nuclear Shelter Underground?"

| # | Claim in video | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "A nuclear explosion produces a blast wave, heat, and radioactive fallout." | Accurate — three distinct hazards (blast pressure/wind, thermal radiation, fallout). | Ready.gov, CDC, ICRP |
| 2 | "Underground sheltering reduces some hazards but does not eliminate them." | Accurate — sheltering reduces exposure; no improvised shelter guarantees survival. | Ready.gov, CDC |
| 3 | "Principle 1 — Distance: farther from blast = lower pressure." | Accurate — blast effects attenuate with distance; fallout zone 10-20 miles. | FEMA Buddemeier |
| 4 | "Principle 2 — Mass: ~3 feet of earth (or equivalent) affords strong protection against gamma radiation." | Accurate (as a general rule of thumb from civil-defense literature). | ORAU TB-5-3 |
| 5 | "Principle 3 — Protected entrance: two right-angle turns scatter radiation." | Accurate per shelter design guidance. | ORAU TB-5-3 |
| 6 | "Principle 4 — Ventilation: filtered air; without it, suffocation risk; ~5 cfm/person." | Accurate (civil-defense ventilation guidance). | ORAU TB-5-3 |
| 7 | "Principle 5 — Time: 80% of fallout radiation decays within 24 hours." | Accurate. | FEMA Buddemeier |
| 8 | "No simple shelter guarantees survival." | Accurate — official guidance never promises survival; sheltering improves odds. | Ready.gov, CDC |

## Deliberate simplifications (educational, not engineering)
- No exact structural dimensions, rebar specs, or blast-overpressure thresholds are given (per safety policy).
- The "3 feet of earth" figure is presented as a general civil-defense rule of thumb, not a construction spec.
- The decay curve is illustrative (80% in 24 h), not a precise isotope decay model.
- Video is a creative educational visualization, NOT a construction tutorial.

## Avoided claims
- No survival percentages.
- No "nuclear-proof" claims.
- No instructions for building a shelter.
- No DIY engineering dimensions.
