# Scientific Research Sources — "Nuclear Shelter Underground?"

## Primary sources
1. Ready.gov — Radiation Emergencies: https://www.ready.gov/radiation
   - "Get inside, stay inside, stay tuned." Walls block radiation; put as many walls between you and outside.
   - Nuclear detonation: large deadly blast; ground burst sucks material up → fallout (sand-like radioactive particles).
   - If warned: take cover from blast; if outside, lie face down to protect skin from heat/debris; after shockwave, go inside quickly.
   - After detonation: 10+ minutes to find shelter before fallout arrives; multi-story building or basement if reachable within minutes; safest = brick/concrete walls; underground parking/subways good shelter.
   - Shelter at least 24 hours unless threatened by immediate hazard or told safe to leave.
   - Radiation levels decrease rapidly, significantly less dangerous in first 24 hours.
2. CDC — Preparing for a Radiation Emergency: https://www.cdc.gov/radiation-emergencies/safety/
   - Sheltering in place: get inside; basement or middle of building; radioactive material settles on outside of buildings → stay as far from walls/roof as possible.
   - Walls block much harmful radiation.
3. ICRP — Advice for the Public on Protection in Case of a Nuclear Detonation: https://www.icrp.org/protection-nuclear-attack.html
   - Go inside center of building/basement; put as much material between you and radioactive material outside.
   - First 10 min / 24 h / 48 h phases. Fallout arrives ~10 min after detonation. Radiation decays rapidly.
   - Decontamination: remove outer contaminated clothing, wipe/wash exposed skin/hair.
   - Food/drink/medicine in shelter safe. Stay tuned to media.
   - If outdoors: move to shelter or behind solid object; avoid direct visibility from point of explosion; don't try to escape by car.
   - If indoors: avoid doors/windows (blast wave hazard).
4. FEMA / Buddemeier (PrepTalk discussion guide): https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_preptalks_buddemeier_discussion-guide.pdf
   - Ground burst: fireball shoots up, pulls thousands of tons of dirt/debris → mushroom cloud; radioactive material mixes with dirt → fallout.
   - Fallout extends 10-20 miles; significant fallout. People have 10-15 min to get inside before fallout begins.
   - Radiation decays rapidly: ~80% of fallout energy released within first 24 hours; ~50% in first hour.
   - Sheltering best protective action; multistory brick building prevents significant exposures; protection factor ~10 for a frame building, ~200 for center of large building.
   - Shelter 12-24 hours; stay tuned.
5. ORAU TB-5-3 — Family Shelters for Protection Against Radioactive Fallout: https://www.orau.org/.../tb-5-3-family-shelters-for-protection-against-rad-fallout.pdf
   - Shielding: earth cover ~3 feet (or equivalent mass) affords high degree of protection against gamma radiation.
   - Entranceways: radiation must make at least two right-angle turns before entering main chamber (scatter).
   - Ventilation: underground shelters need mechanical blowers; 5 cfm/person desirable; avoid fuel-burning apparatus; don't operate blower >2 h without ventilation or hazardous air conditions.
   - Plan for up to 2-week occupancy (conservative).
6. Red Cross — Nuclear Explosion & Radiation Emergencies: https://www.redcross.org/get-help/how-to-prepare-for-emergencies/types-of-emergencies/nuclear-explosion-radiation-emergencies.html
   - Stay inside at least 24 hours unless told otherwise.
